**GitHub Issue**: #106
**Track**: Full SDD
**Predecessor**: gh105 (`predicate-wiring`), which repaired the `jca_android` set this change measures, and gh104 (`legible-violation-reports`), which built the differential trace harness that is this component's behavioural counterpart.
**Reference material (Phase 0, seven rounds)**: `docs/20260821_conformidade_mop_crysl.md` (the plan, 2 441 lines, carrying `(R5)`/`(R6)`/`(R7)` repair marks); `docs/20260824_adjudicacao_plano_conformidade.md` (28 adjudicated decisions and what corpus movement invalidated); `docs/20260824_medicoes_pre_change_conformidade.md` (the two prerequisite measurements, plus the log of the 25 `(R7)` repairs); `docs/20260822_adjudicacao_revisoes_externas.md` §1 — **the list of claims that must NOT be "corrected"**, because they converged across independent reviewers and were still wrong; `docs/20260822_verificacao_consistencia_conformidade.md` (six-dimension consistency report); `docs/20260824_mapeamento_mop_crysl.md` (the JavaMOP ↔ CrySL mapping matrix in both directions, with every construction's status and the enumerated losses — the study this proposal's confidence claim rests on). Reproducible harness: `docs/handoff/20260824_arnes_adjudicacao/` (six Java probes, three scripts, four authored control traces, raw outputs).

## Why

The `jca_android` specification set was translated **by hand** from CrySL rules, and nothing checks that the translation says what the rule requires. Without that check, translation infidelity — the noise this project wants to measure — and deliberate Android adaptation — the contribution it wants to claim — are indistinguishable, and every statement about either rests on hand-maintained tables whose judgment columns are, by the producing script's own docstring, "judgment columns **no analyzer can re-derive**" (`scripts/gh105_predicate_graph.py:34-35`), carried forward by copying them out of a previous version of the same file.

The absence has a measured cost. The same comparison has been re-implemented **seven times** in ad-hoc Python — 2 701 lines across seven distinct `md5` sums, none a copy of another — alongside **eleven** distinct ad-hoc CrySL readers (9 224 lines); 14 of the 18 `gh10*` scripts parse `.mop` files by regular expression. Meanwhile the numbers published from that substrate are unstable in a way no reader can detect from the numbers themselves: the predicate-substrate signature of `jca_android` moved five times in four days (`64/21/5` → `47/26/7` → `28/35/12` → `0/45/19` → `0/70/21`), and `predicate_graph.csv` went from 85 data rows to 45 and back to 70. A published scalar with no counting rule and no commit stamp beside it is not checkable, and Phase 0 found four such scalars that no reconstructible rule reproduces (`129` signature lines, `12 of 23` specs absorbing misuse, `10`/`26` overlapping pointcut pairs, `28 of 55` clauses).

This change introduces a component in the `rvsec` Maven reactor that answers the question mechanically, on five metrics — **M0 monitor vitality**, M1 events, M2 order, M3 constraints, M4 predicates — over three corpora that must never be conflated (`CrySL-Rules` upstream — the single oracle — the frozen `jca`, the current `jca_android`), and emitting a **closed, enumerated `Unknown` taxonomy** so that "I could not read it" and "it is not there" never leave through the same door. The comparison is **literal and mechanical**: where a spec diverges from the rule, the component accuses the divergence; adjudicating deliberate Android adaptation versus translation infidelity is the corpus record's job (`data/jca_android/divergence_record.csv`, the gh104/gh105 lineage), never the translator's (researcher decision, 2026-08-24).

**It measures the instrumentation contract; it does not change it.** No `.mop` file is edited, no pipeline step is altered, and nothing about what the monitors accuse changes. That is why the capability is new rather than a modification of `instrumentation`: mixing the instrument with the object it measures inside one spec file is exactly the confusion this component exists to remove.

Two measurements the Phase 0 plan itself declared as prerequisites for opening this change were executed on 2026-08-24 and are closed. The 129 signature lines and the 155 checked signatures were remeasured under **a fresh `CrySLModelReader` per rule** — the configuration the design mandates, rather than the shared reader they were originally taken under — and are **141** and **141** over the abandoned `api30` corpus, while the shared-reader pass reproduces the previously published `155: 131+19+2+3` digit for digit, which calibrates the whole chain. Redone over the upstream oracle (same probe, same rule): **215** resolved signature lines in both modes, the `diff` with and without `android.jar` is empty, and the check against `android.jar` decomposes as `215 = 175 exact + 29 arity-only + 3 absent-class findings in 5 signatures + 6 declared checker limitations (inheritance not followed)`. And the gh104 differential harness was run against the five global specs with four authored negative controls: four of the five have a live monitor, the fifth (`RandomStringPassword`) has no accusation site at all, and the two stream specs are silent on the traces their own authors label `# violating branch`, in both sets — the `IncompleteOperationError` blind spot, measured by execution instead of argued from the grammar.

## What Changes

- **A new Maven aggregate in the sibling `rvsec` reactor: `rvsec-crysl` (parent) plus three children.** One JVM, one process (researcher decision, 2026-08-24). `rvsec-crysl-core` holds the canonical model, the automata and the M0–M4 comparison, and depends on **neither parser** — it may carry a serialization library for the JSON emitter, but importing `javamop` or `CrySLParser` into it is the error the layering exists to prevent; `rvsec-crysl-mop` lifts `.mop` through `javamop.parser.SpecExtractor` and lowers the model back through `MOPSpecFile` → `DumpVisitor`; `rvsec-crysl-crysl` lifts `.crysl` through `CrySLParser 4.0.6`. The parent overrides `guava.version` to `33.5.0-jre` and **must not** override `scala.version` — `2.13.14` kills `ptltl` with `NoClassDefFoundError: scala/Serializable`, and `ptltl` may not be excluded. `slf4j-simple`, which `CrySLParser` pulls in `compile` scope, is excluded. The four poms were verified to build inside the reactor with `main.basedir` resolving.
- **JSON is an output of the canonical model, never an interchange format.** The alternative — three processes stitched by JSON — was rejected: its stated justification was measured false (`javamop` pulls neither Guava nor Soot), a single `<guava.version>` line puts both parsers in one JVM, and one of the three costs it charged ("a read error becomes an exit code instead of a typed `Unknown` item") contradicts this design's own non-negotiable.
- **M0 — monitor vitality — is the first metric, and it refuses.** It answers three questions decidable from artifacts the design already produces (does the spec index? is the accusation site reachable? does the pointcut resolve against `android.jar`?), and a spec it refuses does not reach M1–M4, because an order verdict over a monitor that does not run is empty. M0 absorbs the non-normalized AST checker the round-trip gate already needed — unique ids, formula alphabet ⊆ ids, every declared event reachable, every `@match` paired with a `@fail` — promoted from internal gate to published metric.
- **Comparison runs against a single oracle: the upstream `CrySL-Rules`** (49 rules; 47 parse without any lexical normalization — the two failures are `OAEPParameterSpec.crysl`, which uses the reserved word `alg` as an object name, and `SSLEngine.crysl`, an upstream bug referencing the undeclared event `cp1`). The generated `api30` oracle is abandoned as a method decision, recorded, not measured against: under counting rule R1 it deletes 25 `CONSTRAINTS` clauses across 12 of the 22 paired rules (`−33` net across 16 rules over the full sets), so keeping it as an oracle would understate what the specs must encode. Pairing between specs and rules is **by declared type** (the `SPEC` FQN against the spec's parameter type; the pointcut's declaring type for the two parameterless specs): 22 of 24 pair, the two skips being `IvChainJunction` (a junction, translating no rule) and `RandomStringPassword` (`String` has no rule). Pairing by file name is forbidden — `SecretKeySpec.mop` would match both `SecretKey.crysl` and `SecretKeySpec.crysl`.
- **The event alphabet is not disjoint, and the model is built for it.** `events` is a `List` ordered by declaration (declaration order is dispatch order); `order` is a symbolic automaton over signatures with optional guards; the object compared is the inverse morphism `h⁻¹(L)`, where `h` maps a signature to the concatenation, in declaration order, of every label whose pointcut matches it. `Map<Label, Set<Signature>>` is **forbidden** — one call can emit two letters, measured.
- **Every published witness carries `ABSTRACT` or `CONCRETE`**, plus the list of normalizations applied to reach it. An `ABSTRACT` witness may not be published beside the words "false positive": a word accepted by an automaton is not an executable trace.
- **The `Unknown` taxonomy is closed and enumerated**: `UnrecognizedConstraint{textoCru, site}`, `OverlappingDispatch{labels, signature, site}`, `MultiSlicedOrder{params, site}`, `UnresolvedSignature{signature, class, mode, site}`, `UntranslatableConstraint{clause, family, site}`. A refusal category with no fixed schema is not countable, and the whole reporting discipline depends on it being countable.
- **Every emitted table carries its counting rule and the identity of the artifact it came from.** The canonical model carries a `version` per corpus — repository, commit and read timestamp — because the input spans **two** git repositories (`rvsec`, `rvsec-cognicrypt`) plus one SDK directory. A single scalar commit would stamp an upstream-derived number with a commit of a repository that did not produce it.
- **A calibration gate stands between the component and acceptance**: it must reproduce eight targets before it is trusted, and **every target's route is one the component does not produce**. Two of the eight as first drafted failed that test — pairing "by name" is the rule the component itself applies, and the `MapOfMonitor` census "by AST proxy" is the proxy the component implements, so neither could ever come out wrong; the first now reads the declared skips of `order_alphabet_map.csv` and the second **regenerates the monitors**. A quantity with no independent route is published as a labelled self-consistency check, never counted as calibration. A disagreement is a finding to be adjudicated by measuring both sides, never a reason to tune the component until it agrees.
- **The `mop.lower` round-trip gate**, in two layers with different standing: (1) the non-normalized AST checker, which is cheap, non-circular and catches the two failure modes that language equivalence cannot see; (2) product search kept as *evidence*, with the applied normalizations printed beside each verdict.
- **Ad-hoc code dies, in two stages, on a written criterion**: *the ad-hoc dies when the component reproduces its verdict, not when it compiles.* Six `ORDER` comparators and seven CrySL readers under `audit/20260808_*` move to `backup/` in this change — nothing in CI depends on them. The CI gates (`scripts/gh105_order_gate.py`, the three `gh10{1,4}_*.py` that open `.cryptsl`, and `tests/parity/test_gh105_predicate_gates.py`) survive this change and are retired in a later cleanup change, after the component reproduces their verdicts.
- **Out of scope, with the reason written down**: `crysl.lower` (no known consumer, and ~400 lines of pretty-printer because the CrySL project ships no formatter — `rvsec-crysl-crysl` exists anyway for the lift); and the promotion of the gh104 harness to an M2 oracle, which needs two pieces that do not exist (a word enumerator and an event → call-with-arguments map).

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

- `conformance`: mechanical verification that a JavaMOP specification set encodes what the CrySL rules it was translated from require, on five metrics (M0 vitality, M1 events, M2 order, M3 constraints, M4 predicates), against the upstream CrySL rules as the single oracle, with a closed `Unknown` taxonomy, typed witnesses carrying `ABSTRACT`/`CONCRETE`, and a counting rule plus commit stamp on every emitted table. Invariant prefix `INV-CONF`. This follows the precedent of `aperv`, `calibration-control` and `campaign-analysis` for capabilities outside the seven original domains.

### Modified Capabilities

(none — this change **measures** the instrumentation contract and does not change it. No `.mop` file is edited, no pipeline step is altered, and no monitor accuses differently as a result. `instrumentation` keeps every requirement and invariant it has.)

## Impact

- **Java (rvsec reactor, sibling tree)** — the whole implementation lives here: new `rvsec/rvsec-crysl/` with four `pom.xml` (parent + `-core` + `-mop` + `-crysl`), registered in `rvsec/rvsec/pom.xml` `<modules>`. New dependency `de.darmstadt.tu.crossing.CrySL:CrySLParser:4.0.6` in the `-crysl` child only, with `slf4j-simple` excluded and `guava.version` overridden to `33.5.0-jre` in the component's parent pom; the reactor's `scala.version` is inherited unchanged. `rvsec-mop-extractor` serves as the **shape** template (pom, CLI, facade, visitor, writer), not as code to copy. The reactor build command and JDK constraints are unchanged.
- **rv-android modules**: none of the 16 Python modules changes behaviour. Effects are confined to `scripts/` (six comparators and seven readers under `audit/20260808_*` move to `backup/`; the CI gate scripts are untouched by this change) and to `data/jca_android/` (the component becomes the producer of `predicate_graph.csv`, `constraint_table.csv`, `order_alphabet_map.csv` and `divergence_record.csv` rather than their consumer, emitting to the same schemas).
- **Corpora, read-only**: `rvsec/rvsec-mop/src/main/resources/{jca,jca_android,jca_android_bug_predicate,generic,generic_new}` (215 `.mop`), `rvsec-cognicrypt/CrySL-Rules/` (49 rules, 47 effective). Rule defects become recorded findings, never upstream edits.
- **Cross-module interfaces**: none added. The component is a batch CLI over files; it does not enter the instrumentation pipeline, the platform, or any experiment path.
- **CI**: `.github/workflows/ci.yml` gains a step running the four new modules' tests. Without it they never run — the reactor build step passes `-DskipTests` (`:30`) and only dexlib2 `grammar-tests` opts back in (`:44`) — and the component's entire contract is Java tests. The oracle-dependent subset is tagged and excluded there, because `rvsec-cognicrypt` and `$ANDROID_HOME` are outside the CI checkout. Both points are `risk-register.md` RISK-001 and RISK-002.
- **FRs/NFRs**: FR01 (monitor generation from JavaMOP specifications — the pipeline whose input this component audits), FR03 (specification-set support — the sets `jca` and `jca_android` are the measured subjects), NFR03 (testability — the calibration gate and the round-trip gate are the component's own test contract), NFR06 (observability — counting rule plus commit stamp on every emitted table, and the closed `Unknown` taxonomy that makes refusals countable).
- **Constraints inherited from Phase 0**: the corpus is a moving target (`jca_android` changed in every prior round; it now stands at 24 specs with gh105 at 72 of 74 and the remaining two blocked on gh104 archival), which is why `version` is a model requirement; and the `CrySLModelReader` leaks `OBJECTS` scope between rules read by the same reader in both directions, which is why a fresh reader per rule is a correctness requirement and not a style preference.
